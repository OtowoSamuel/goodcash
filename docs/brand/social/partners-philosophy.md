# Warm Ledger

Money made friendly through weight and outline. Every form is drawn as if inked by hand and then set down hard on the page: a thick black contour, a solid shadow pushed down and to the right, never blurred. Nothing floats in a haze; everything sits, heavy and certain, like a coin placed on a counter. The work should look meticulously crafted, each outline the same weight, each shadow the same offset, the product of painstaking attention rather than a template.

Space is the calm in the composition. A single field of soft lilac holds everything, and the generous empty room around each card is as deliberate as the cards themselves. Margins are wide and even. Elements never crowd or touch the edge; they are placed with master-level care so the eye moves in one clear path, from the name of the house, to the headline, to the partners, to the address.

Colour is a small, strict vocabulary. Lilac for ground, ink for every line and letter, white and mint for the cards that carry names, yellow for the one thing to act on, purple for the second coin. No gradients, no glow, no texture: flat colour, calibrated so each one has a job. The restraint is the craft.

Type is chunky and kind. One heavy, rounded grotesque speaks in large, tight lines; a plain, quiet sans carries the small print. Words are few and each one earns its place. Scale does the talking: a headline you read from across a room, and labels you read up close.

Two overlapping coins are the quiet signature: one gold, one violet, one in front of the other. They stand for the passage between two kinds of money, and they appear once, small and exact, so that people who know the mark recognise it at once. Every detail is the result of careful refinement: master-level execution in a cheerful voice.
